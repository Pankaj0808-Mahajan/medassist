# Common blood test values (educational summary)

HbA1c reflects average glucose over about three months. Typical non-diabetic values are below 5.7 percent. Fasting glucose is normally 70 to 99 mg/dL. High values need confirmation and review by a doctor.

Hemoglobin below about 12 g/dL in women or 13 g/dL in men suggests anemia, which has many causes such as iron deficiency, blood loss or chronic disease. Very low hemoglobin (below 7 g/dL) needs urgent care. A high white blood cell count can accompany infection or inflammation. A low platelet count raises bleeding risk, and very low counts need urgent evaluation.

Creatinine is a waste product filtered by the kidneys. A high creatinine can indicate reduced kidney function, dehydration or certain medicines, and should be reviewed with a doctor, who may also look at the estimated glomerular filtration rate and urine tests. Potassium and sodium are electrolytes: values far outside 3.5-5.1 mmol/L for potassium or 135-145 mmol/L for sodium can affect heart rhythm and brain function and need urgent attention when extreme.

Cholesterol: an LDL cholesterol below 100 mg/dL, total cholesterol below 200 mg/dL, HDL above 40 mg/dL and triglycerides below 150 mg/dL are usual targets for adults at average risk. Lifestyle measures such as diet, physical activity and not smoking are the first steps; whether medicine is needed is decided by a doctor based on overall cardiovascular risk. Raised ALT or AST can reflect liver irritation from fatty liver, alcohol, viral hepatitis or medicines and is followed up by a clinician.
