# Pneumonia and chest X-rays (educational summary)

Pneumonia is an infection of the lungs. Common symptoms are cough with phlegm, fever, chills, fast or difficult breathing and chest pain when breathing or coughing. A chest X-ray can show areas of increased density in the lungs that are consistent with pneumonia, but an X-ray alone does not make the diagnosis. Doctors combine the image with symptoms, examination, oxygen levels and sometimes blood tests.

A computer model reading an X-ray can miss disease or flag healthy lungs, and its confidence score is not the same as the probability that a person has pneumonia. Every image must be read by a qualified clinician. Heatmaps show which parts of the image influenced the model, not the cause of any finding.

Seek urgent medical care for difficulty breathing, blue or grey lips, confusion, chest pain, a high fever that does not settle, or if the person is an infant, an older adult or has a weak immune system or long-term lung or heart disease. Rest, fluids and finishing any prescribed treatment are important, and antibiotics are only appropriate when prescribed by a doctor.
